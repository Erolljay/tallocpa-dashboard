New tables (for the developer)

  • workpaper_files: client, period (month or quarter), status, locked_at
  • workpaper_schedules: file, ref (A–T), kind, prepared_by/at, reviewed_by/at
  • workpaper_adjustments: schedule, kind, amount, return line, reason, support document, created_by
  • workpaper_ticks: schedule, row key, tick (agreed to source, traced to ledger)
  • review_notes: file, schedule ref, row key, body, status, raised_by, cleared_by, reply

Schedules are computed, like worksheets today. Only sign-offs, ticks, adjustments and notes are stored. The file locks with the last return of the period, the same way worksheets lock now.

Email intake: invoices@tallocpa.com

  • The sender is matched to a client: a client user's email, or a sender the client adds (e.g. their bookkeeper). Someone linked to several businesses adds the client code: invoices+JAS@tallocpa.com.
  • Each attachment (PDF, JPG, PNG) becomes a document with source Email, tagged to a period by its date and the received time, with the same cutoff rules as the portal (§4.2).
  • The sender gets a receipt: how many files were received and a portal link. It never repeats the contents.
  • Unknown senders, and mail that fails sender checks (SPF/DKIM), go to an Unmatched list for staff to assign or reject.
  • n8n watches the mailbox and passes attachments to the app, so it stays plumbing only (Q11).

Batch upload (later, at onboarding)

  • An Excel template in the register layout, your worksheet columns. For the opening months, backlog, or clients with their own POS or accounting system.
  • Every row is checked before anything posts, like the payroll register preview today: TIN format, VAT at 12% within rounding, duplicate invoice numbers, open period, known account title.
  • Rows post as register lines marked “Batch · no image”. Images can be attached later; the reviewer sees how many lines have none.
  • The workpapers generate from these lines the same way.

Never missing a client's deadline

Nobody forgets that the 1601-C is due on the 10th. A deadline is missed when the work behind it stalls and nobody notices: the payroll register never came in, the preparer was on leave, the client didn't approve, the funds weren't there. So the control watches each step, not only the BIR date.

What the app already does

  • Creates every filing 120 days ahead each night from the client's obligations.
  • Moves a due date that falls on a weekend or holiday to the next working day.
  • Lists overdue filings and the next 14 days on the dashboard, and 60 days on Deadlines.
  • Requests documents from clients on the 1st, reminds them, and flags missed cutoffs.
  • Locks the books and payroll month once the return is filed.

What's missing

  • No staff member is named on a filing. The Deadlines table shows due date, client, form, period and status only.
  • No internal target date ahead of the BIR date.
  • No steps with dates, so “Not started” three days out looks the same as a month out.
  • No reminders or escalation to staff, the reviewer or you. The nightly job reminds clients only.
  • Nothing checks that a client with payroll or withholding has the obligation, or that every filing has a preparer.

Each person's routine

Preparer. Opens My work: their filings by the next step's date, red first. When stuck, marks the filing “waiting on client” with a reason, which also sends the client a request.

Reviewer. Reviews At risk and Critical filings on their clients first.

Owner. Opens the Deadlines tracker: every client and form due this month in one grid. Assigns preparers to gaps, approves what waits on you, and calls clients who are Critical.

Email goes through the nightly job and Resend that already exist (§3.13). This adds checks to the deadline engine, not a new system.
